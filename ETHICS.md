# Ethical use, attribution and evidence commitments

CloudSentry is for customer-controlled defensive assessment, finite deception, evidence preservation and descriptive analysis of explicitly selected material. Permission is specific to an operation and scope. A visitor connection, suspicious pattern, imported authorization claim or failed detector is not permission to act on another person's systems.

## Protect the customer, not punish an endpoint

Apply resource controls to infrastructure you own or are authorized to administer. Bound CPU, memory, processes, storage/device I/O, accepted bytes, lifetime and logging through independently reviewed controls. Do not create amplification, circular forwarding, callbacks, forced remote workloads, indefinite holds, OCR sabotage or a loop intended to consume somebody else's listener. Incoming bytes are data, never commands or administrative approval.

No GPU workload is granted by the echo. A CPU quota is not a hypervisor, and a GPU timeslice is not automatically an isolation boundary. Pressure or baseline deviation can justify investigation but does not establish mining, cracking, inference, exfiltration, identity or intent. Do not silently relax an unavailable control to make a deployment succeed.

## Keep evidence distinct from conclusions

Preserve exact source hashes, timestamps and packet/session provenance where the feature supports them. Keep original evidence separately. Hashes and separately retained pins support consistency checks, not proof of initial truth, identity, legal authority or guilt. Mark synthetic state, uploaded declarations, unobserved controls and coverage gaps explicitly. A successful bounded echo or apparent virtual root prompt is not proof of unauthorized access to a real customer machine.

Customer-selected writing summaries describe linguistic/content patterns. They are not psychological dossiers, authorship matches, health assessments, protected-trait inference or identity attribution. Do not publish private text, incident evidence, credential-bearing configurations or names merely to make a public repository larger.

## Preserve creators and original work

The root README credits referenced creators and communities through profile/project links. Their names are attribution, not claims of partnership, endorsement or involvement. Retain component-specific licenses, copyright, original documentation and source revision notices. The root Apache-2.0 license does not replace an upstream license; the previous root MIT notice and existing grants are preserved as described in NOTICE.

The configuration auditor from HuginnsHammer is a separately namespaced, pinned MIT-licensed source integration. Other linked VM, firmware, hardware, TPM and research projects are references or reviewed distribution tools unless a component's notice explicitly says otherwise. Do not silently copy access-gated corpora or treat a GitHub link as authorization to deploy its defaults.

Creator credits, handle references and professional networking are welcome. Direct messages, issues, pull requests, public evidence submissions and third-party contributions are separate from attribution and are not automatically sent. Contributions can be prepared for customer review without claiming they were accepted or published elsewhere.

## Be accurate about readiness

A clean scan is not proof of security. A failed or incomplete scan is not clean. Static admission does not prove runtime containment. Functional software-TPM, firmware or image tests do not certify physical hardware, a customer's production isolation or a cloud-spending ceiling. Local test success is not a completed public CI run. Explicitly retain `unknown`, partial and deployment-blocked states until independent evidence supports a narrower claim.
